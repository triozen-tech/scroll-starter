How to research a business type

Do this by hand, in your own browser, before you design anything. Go section by section. For each section, collect three or four examples, then write down what you would borrow. Do not copy a layout: mix ideas from several places so the result is yours.

Rules for yourself. Never copy a brand's logo, name or exact layout. Never use another site's pictures. Keep your screenshots in a private folder and out of the project.

Before you start. Write the business in one line, then list the sections you expect (start from the ones below and remove what does not fit). Aim for nine to twelve sections.

Hero

What it is for. The first screen. It says what the business is in one glance and makes you want to scroll.

What to look for.

  • How big is the headline, and how many words?
  • What moves on the first second: a clip, a product, text?
  • Where is the menu, and how loud is it?
  • Is there one clear button?

Navigation and loader

What it is for. How a visitor finds their way, and what they see while the page loads.

What to look for.

  • Is the menu a bar, a pill, a side drawer or a full-screen overlay?
  • What changes on hover?
  • Does the loader show the name, a counter or a picture?

Story or craft section

What it is for. Why this business is different. Often the most cinematic part of the page.

What to look for.

  • Is it a video, a pinned scene or a long sticky image?
  • How does the text appear: line by line, word by word, all at once?
  • How long does the visitor scroll before something new happens?

Product or service showcase

What it is for. Shows what is for sale, big and close.

What to look for.

  • How does one card look and what does it do on hover?
  • Can you flip, zoom, swap colour or see another angle?
  • How many items at once, and how is the grid shaped?

Shop details

What it is for. Prices, options and the buy button. Where a visitor decides.

What to look for.

  • How are options chosen: chips, swatches, a dropdown?
  • What happens when you add to the cart?
  • Is there a bar that stays with you while you scroll?

What it is for. Pictures that make the brand feel real.

What to look for.

  • Grid, slider, stack or full-bleed?
  • How does an image enlarge?
  • Do the pictures share one colour mood?

Social proof

What it is for. Why a stranger should trust this business. For your concept site, write clearly invented sample quotes and label them as samples.

What to look for.

  • Quotes, logos, numbers or ratings?
  • How does it move: marquee, stack, fade?
  • How much of the page does it take?

What to look for.

  • What is the button's wording?
  • How does it confirm?

Footer

What it is for. The tidy end of the page, with the links people look for.

What to look for.

  • Big name, columns of links, or a stack of cards?
  • Is there a final move, such as text sliding in?
  • Where is the credit line?
